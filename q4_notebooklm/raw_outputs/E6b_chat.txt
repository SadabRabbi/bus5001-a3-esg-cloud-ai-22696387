The uploaded sources show that the University of Melbourne did not abandon its goal of carbon neutrality by 2025 in its 2022 report.
In fact, throughout the 2022 report, the University of Melbourne reaffirms its commitment to achieving Climate Active certified carbon neutrality by 2025[1][2]:

* Target Status: The report explicitly states that the University is *"on track to achieve carbon neutrality by 2025"*[3].
* Baseline Established: It reports using its 2022 comprehensive Greenhouse Gas Inventory (GHGI) compiled by Pangolin Associates as the baseline to take direct action toward the 2025 target[1][4].
* Milestone Progression: The 2025 carbon neutral target is framed as a prerequisite step before achieving its broader 2030 climate positive commitment[2].

Source of Potential Confusion
The decision to step away from Climate Active carbon neutrality certification was made by RMIT University in its 2025 report (not the University of Melbourne)[5]. In 2025, RMIT reviewed its carbon management approach and gained approval to transition away from Climate Active carbon neutrality certification in order to adopt an international science-based net zero pathway[5][6].
