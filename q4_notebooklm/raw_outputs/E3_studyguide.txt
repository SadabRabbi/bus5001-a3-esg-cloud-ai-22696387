Module 1: Strategic Governance and Institutional Architecture

1.1 Guiding Strategic Frameworks

The University of Melbourne’s sustainability operations are anchored by three core strategic documents:

1. Sustainability Plan 2030 (SP2030): Launched in May 2022, SP2030 establishes an eight-year roadmap structured across three core domains (Amplifying action, Mobilising knowledge, and Walking the talk).
2. Sustainability Charter: Defines the overarching principles and institutional commitments to environmental stewardship.
3. Advancing Melbourne: The master institutional strategy, ensuring sustainability is integrated across teaching, research, global engagement, and place-making.

1.2 Administrative and Operational Governance

Governance structures ensure cross-portfolio accountability and delivery of sustainability targets:

* Sustainability Advisory Group (SAG): Convened in 2022 to guide the implementation of SP2030, replacing the former Sustainability Executive (which directed the Sustainability Plan 2017–2020). Chaired by senior leaders, SAG comprises executives, academic/professional staff, and student representatives, meeting quarterly to advise the Chief Operating Officer (Paul Axup).
* Sustainability Strategy Team (Chancellery / CFO Group): Led by Katie Mee; responsible for strategic direction, SP2030 coordination, performance tracking, and formal ESG reporting.
* Sustainability Team (Business Services / COO Portfolio): Led by Sue Hopkins; manages operational sustainability across campuses, including energy, water, waste, transport, biodiversity, and sustainable event logistics.

* Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs): Contracted with the Murra Warra and Crowlands wind farms, purchasing 67 GWh of electricity in 2022.
* Large-scale Generation Certificates (LGCs): Voluntarily retired 49,575 LGCs (sourced primarily from the wind farm PPAs) to ensure 64% of total electricity consumed was directly matched with renewable sources.
* On-Campus Solar Generation: Installed across 44 buildings, comprising over 11,000 PV panels. Generates over 3 GWh of clean energy annually (saving ~3,000 tonnes CO₂-e; equivalent to powering 650 homes).

4.2 Energy Efficiency and Electrification Initiatives

* Smart Campus Energy Upgrades: Invested $6 million in LED lighting, chiller replacements, PV expansion, and HVAC optimization. Yields annual savings of 3.8 GWh (~820 homes powered).
* Gas-to-Electricity Decarbonization Program:
  * Following a 2021 audit of Parkville gas assets, a prioritization matrix was established to eliminate natural gas.
  * 2022 Pilots: Replaced 13 gas-fired domestic hot water units across 10 buildings with electric heat pumps.
  * Scoping commenced for full electrification of the Burnley campus.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Question 1: What reporting frameworks and standards govern the University of Melbourne’s sustainability reporting?

Answer: The University of Melbourne structures its disclosures around the Reporting Principles of the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI), while integrating draft standards for climate and sustainability financial disclosures from the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB). Operations are managed under an ISO 14001:2015 compliant Environmental Management System (EMS), and greenhouse gas accounting is structured according to the Climate Active standard. Additionally, activities are aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, pp. 7, 8)

Question 2: What are the primary carbon targets established by the University of Melbourne?

Answer: The University is committed to achieving Climate Active Carbon Neutral Certification by 2025 across all operational scopes. Following 2025, the University aims to achieve Climate Positive status by 2030. Net zero emissions for electricity were achieved in 2021 and maintained through 2022. (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, pp. 5, 13)

Question 3: How does the University of Melbourne source its electricity, and what percentage is renewable?

Answer: In 2022, the University purchased 67 GWh of electricity through Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) with the Murra Warra and Crowlands wind farms. By voluntarily surrendering 49,575 Large-scale Generation Certificates (LGCs), the University ensured 64% of its overall electricity consumption came directly from renewable sources. On campus, over 11,000 solar PV panels across 44 buildings generate over 3 GWh of clean power annually. (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, pp. 13, 14)

Question 4: How are Scope 3 indirect emissions accounted for, and what are the largest contributors?

Answer: In 2022, the University expanded its Greenhouse Gas Inventory (GHGI) by commissioning Pangolin Associates to analyze over $1 billion in supplier procurement expenditure. Total Scope 3 emissions reached 168,192 tCO₂-e (76.6% of gross emissions). The single largest Scope 3 category is Professional Services (40,413 tCO₂-e), followed by Construction and Repair Services (39,293 tCO₂-e), Products, Materials and Equipment (22,839 tCO₂-e), and Business Air Travel (15,581 tCO₂-e). Upfront construction emissions were piloted using life cycle analyses (LCAs) from the Student Precinct Project. (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, p. 13)

Question 5: What carbon offset projects were utilized to balance the 2022 carbon inventory?

Answer: The University retired offsets totaling 39,003 tCO₂-e to balance Scope 2 electricity emissions and 15,581 tCO₂-e for business air travel. Offset sources included Victorian Biogas to Energy (19,683 ACCUs), Hydropower in Indonesia (12,395 VCS), Biomass in China (2,975 VCS), Eco Australia conservation (2,479 ABU/Gold Standard), Wollert Landfill Gas in Victoria (991 ACCUs), Nullawarra Station Forest Protection in NSW (317 ACCUs), and Zhangbei Wind Power in China (163 tCO₂-e for electricity; 15,581 tCO₂-e for flights). An additional 754 tCO₂-e was purchased from Greenfleet for vehicle fleet offsetting. (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, p. 14)

Question 6: What strategy is in place to replace natural gas infrastructure across campuses?

Answer: Natural gas combustion represents the University's primary Scope 1 footprint (15,098 tCO₂-e). Following a 2021 audit of Parkville gas assets, a prioritization matrix was established to guide a multi-year electrification rollout across complex heating precincts. In 2022, pilot projects replaced 13 gas-fired hot water units across 10 buildings with electric heat pumps, and planning commenced to fully electrify the Burnley campus. (Source: University of Melbourne Sustainability Report 2022, p. 13)
